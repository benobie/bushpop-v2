# Best Op Shops in Adelaide

Updated 2026. Adelaide packs a lot into a compact city, with the East End and Rundle
Street holding one of the best vintage strips in the country alongside a strong
charity-shop network. This is where to thrift across the city, area by area, with
addresses and a note on what each store does best. Every shop below was checked for
2026.

## The East End and Rundle Street

The densest run of vintage and curated secondhand in Adelaide, all walkable.

- **Midwest Trader** (Shops 1 and 2, 4-10 Ebenezer Pl, Adelaide). An East End
  institution of more than 27 years. Best for American vintage workwear, band tees,
  cowboy boots and denim.
- **Filter Store** (Shop 5, 4-10 Ebenezer Pl, Adelaide). Avant-garde Japanese and
  European designer alongside vintage military workwear. Best for Comme des Garçons,
  Issey Miyake, Margiela and collector pieces.
- **Goodbyes** (206 Rundle St, Adelaide). A consignment store turning over 800 to
  1,000 hand-picked pieces a week. Best for curated Australian and international
  labels like Gorman and Zimmermann.
- **RSPCA Op Shop Rundle Street** (238A Rundle St, Adelaide). The RSPCA's
  designer-focused charity store. Best for Gucci, Prada and Burberry at charity
  prices.
- **Little Ms Waste-Not Vintage** (238 Rundle St, Adelaide). Museum-quality vintage
  from the 1920s to '80s, sourced from original owners with real provenance. Hours
  are limited, so check before visiting. Best for authentic, documented vintage.
- **Red Cross Op Shop** (284 Rundle St, Adelaide). A charity store with extended
  hours in a prime East End spot. Best for everyday secondhand.
- **Dulcie's Vintage** (Shop 15 GL, Adelaide Arcade, Adelaide). A charity store with
  new vintage stock arriving daily, supporting local services. Best for a quick daily
  dig.
- **My Dead Grandpa** (10-16 Ranelagh St, Adelaide). A 900sqm vintage market of more
  than 50 vendors, every item at least 15 years old. Best for a one-stop vintage
  browse across clothing, vinyl and furniture.

## North Adelaide

- **Sharehouse** (North Adelaide Railway Station, War Memorial Dr, North Adelaide). A
  vintage store in an 1857 heritage railway station, with vinyl, coffee and rotating
  art. Opened in 2026, open Wednesday to Saturday. Best for vintage in an unusual
  setting.

## Norwood and The Parade

The eastern suburbs' donations make The Parade a reliable hunt.

- **Vinnies Norwood** (70 The Parade, Norwood). A character-filled charity store in a
  former bank, known for vintage, retro and designer donations. Best for a long dig.
- **Save the Children Norwood** (63 The Parade, Norwood). A charity store cited for
  its large selection of American vintage. Best for vintage on the strip.

## Prospect and the inner north

- **Savers** (Shop 4, 252 Churchill Rd, Prospect). A thrift superstore and the
  largest dedicated footprint in the inner north. Best for high-volume hunting across
  every category.

## Unley and Clarence Park

- **Aggies Op Shop** (183 Unley Rd, Unley). A well-organised charity store in an
  original church building. Best for curated, well-priced preloved.
- **Chaos Bazaar Vintage** (306 Goodwood Rd, Clarence Park). A specialist vintage
  store of 1920s to '80s clothing and accessories, no fast fashion. Open Friday and
  Saturday. Best for serious vintage hunters.

## Port Adelaide, Semaphore and the coast

- **Vinnies Port Adelaide** (91 Commercial Rd, Port Adelaide). A charity store on the
  Commercial Road strip. Best for preloved fashion and homewares.
- **Worn Again** (24 Quebec St, Port Adelaide, and 31 Brighton Rd, Glenelg). A
  community not-for-profit running a near-flat four-dollar pricing model. Best for
  ultra-budget clothing, shoes and books.
- **Trashville Boutique and Vintage** (115A Semaphore Rd, Semaphore). A Semaphore Road
  institution of quirky retro fashion and decor. Best for the suburb's most
  distinctive vintage stop.
- **RSPCA Op Shop Semaphore** (106 Semaphore Rd, Semaphore). A charity store on the
  main strip. Best for an affordable browse alongside Trashville.

## What to expect

Prices range from a flat few dollars at the budget charity stores up to premium for
authenticated designer and museum-grade vintage. Charity shops restock through the
week, and several vintage stores keep limited hours, so it is worth checking before
a special trip. Quality varies by suburb and by week.

## Tips for finding deals

- **Do the East End in one loop.** Ebenezer Place and Rundle Street pack the best
  vintage and curated stores within a few minutes' walk.
- **Go midweek** for fresh stock and quieter stores.
