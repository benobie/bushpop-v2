# Fabric Care Guide

Every fabric has its own rules, and getting them wrong is how a good garment
gets ruined in one wash. This guide covers five fabrics you will see constantly
in secondhand wardrobes: wool, denim, silk, leather and synthetics. For the
symbols on the label itself, see our [washing symbols decoder](/guides/washing-symbols/).
If a care label is missing, matching the fabric by feel and following the
guidance below is the safest approach.

## Wool

Wool felts and shrinks permanently if it gets too hot or too much agitation, so
gentleness is the whole game.

- **Washing.** Hand wash, or use a wool/delicate machine cycle, in water no
  warmer than 30°C. Use a mild detergent made for wool, ideally a liquid rather
  than a powder. Swish the garment gently through the water; never rub, twist or
  wring it.
- **Drying.** Never tumble dry wool: the heat and tumbling action felt and
  shrink the fibres. Press out excess water in a towel, then dry the garment
  flat on a clean towel, reshaping it while damp, out of direct sunlight.
- **Ironing.** If needed, iron on a low-to-medium wool setting with a damp
  pressing cloth between the iron and the garment, or use a steamer instead.
- **Common mistakes.** Washing in hot water, tumble drying, and hanging wet
  knitwear on a hanger or line, which stretches the fibres out of shape under
  their own weight.
- **Storage.** Fold wool jumpers rather than hanging them, to stop the shoulders
  stretching. Store with cedar blocks or lavender sachets rather than mothballs,
  in a breathable space, not sealed plastic.

## Denim

Denim holds its shape and colour best when it is washed as little as
practically possible.

- **Washing.** Wash cold, inside out, on a gentle cycle, and only every five to
  ten wears rather than after every wear. Frequent washing is the main thing
  that fades colour and breaks down the fit over time.
- **Drying.** Air dry where you can, ideally by laying flat or hanging out of
  direct sun. A brief low-heat tumble dry is fine if you want to soften stiff
  denim, but high heat repeatedly shrinks and fades jeans.
- **Ironing.** Denim tolerates a hot iron well, but iron inside out to protect
  any printed or coated finishes.
- **Common mistakes.** Washing after every wear, using hot water, and high-heat
  tumble drying every time, all of which fade colour and shrink the fit faster
  than the fabric otherwise would.
- **Storage.** Hang or fold either works. Keep dark or raw denim out of direct
  sunlight for long stretches, since UV fades indigo dye unevenly.

## Silk

Silk is a protein fibre, closer to hair than to cotton, and it reacts badly to
heat, harsh detergent and direct sun.

- **Washing.** Hand wash in cold to lukewarm water with a dedicated silk or
  delicate wash, not a regular detergent, which is too harsh on the fibres.
  Soak briefly, no more than about 30 minutes, and gently press the water
  through the fabric rather than rubbing or scrubbing.
- **Drying.** Roll the item in a towel to blot out excess water rather than
  wringing it, then dry flat or on a padded hanger, away from direct sunlight,
  which can yellow and weaken silk over time.
- **Ironing.** Iron on the lowest setting while the fabric is still slightly
  damp, on the reverse side, or use a pressing cloth. High heat can scorch silk
  almost instantly.
- **Common mistakes.** Using regular laundry detergent, washing in hot water,
  wringing out water, and drying in direct sun.
- **Storage.** Store on padded hangers or folded with acid-free tissue paper,
  away from humidity and direct light.

## Leather

Leather is skin, not woven fabric, and it should never go anywhere near a
washing machine.

- **Cleaning.** Never machine wash or submerge leather. Wipe down dust and
  surface grime with a soft, dry cloth. For marks, use a barely damp cloth with
  a little mild soap, blotting rather than rubbing, and avoid soaking the
  surface.
- **Drying.** If leather gets wet, air dry it away from direct heat, such as a
  radiator, heater or direct sun, all of which dry it out too fast and can
  cause cracking.
- **Conditioning.** Apply a leather conditioner every six months or so, worked
  in gently with a soft cloth in small circular motions. This keeps the leather
  supple and helps prevent cracking as it ages.
- **Common mistakes.** Machine washing or soaking leather, using household
  products like vinegar or baking soda to clean it (both strip the leather's
  natural oils), and storing it in sealed plastic, which traps moisture.
- **Storage.** Use a wide, padded hanger that supports the shoulders and keeps
  the garment's shape. Store in a cool, dry place out of direct sun, and cover
  with a breathable cotton garment bag rather than plastic.

## Synthetics: polyester, nylon and blends

Synthetic fibres are strong and low-maintenance, but they are sensitive to heat
and to chlorine bleach in ways natural fibres are not.

- **Washing.** Wash in cold to warm water on a synthetic or permanent press
  cycle, with a gentle detergent. Turn garments inside out before washing to
  reduce pilling caused by friction against other items in the wash.
- **Bleach.** Avoid chlorine bleach entirely on polyester and nylon; it breaks
  down the synthetic coating and can cause yellowing. If you need to whiten or
  brighten, use an oxygen-based bleach instead.
- **Drying.** Air dry where possible. If you tumble dry, use a low-heat
  setting: high heat can melt, shrink or permanently set creases into
  synthetic fibres.
- **Common mistakes.** High-heat washing or drying, chlorine bleach, and
  washing synthetics alongside rough fabrics like denim or towelling, which
  accelerates pilling through friction.
- **Storage.** Hang or fold; synthetics have no special humidity or light
  sensitivity, but keep them away from direct heat sources like radiators.
