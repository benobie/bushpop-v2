# Best Op Shops in Ipswich

Updated 2026. Ipswich thrifts in two dense pockets and a scattering of outposts:
a CBD core where Vinnies, Salvos, Lifeline, Red Cross and an Animal Welfare
League shop all sit within a few streets of each other, a Bundamba and Booval
strip that mixes national charity brands with genuine independents, and one or
two shops apiece anchoring the newer growth-corridor centres at Springfield,
Redbank Plains, Goodna, Yamanto and Karalee. This is where to thrift across the
city, precinct by precinct, with addresses and a note on what each store does
best. Every shop below was checked for 2026.

## Ipswich CBD and West Ipswich

Brisbane Street carries the city's densest run of charity shops.

- **Vinnies Ipswich** (272 Brisbane St, West Ipswich). A standard charity op
  shop. Best for an everyday browse at the heart of the Brisbane Street run.
- **Salvos Stores Ipswich** (307 Brisbane St, West Ipswich). The Salvos family
  store, almost directly across the road from Vinnies. Best for pairing with
  Vinnies in a single stop.
- **AWL Qld Op Shop Ipswich** (189 Brisbane St, Ipswich). An Animal Welfare
  League Queensland shop, with proceeds funding animal rescue and rehoming. Best
  for a charity stop that directly supports animal welfare.
- **Lifeline Superstore East Ipswich** (80 Jacaranda St, East Ipswich). A
  larger-format Lifeline outlet. Best for high-volume hunting across every
  category rather than a quick browse.

## North Ipswich and Riverlink

- **Red Cross Shop Ipswich** (32 Downs St, North Ipswich). A standard charity op
  shop. Best for an everyday North Ipswich browse.
- **Lifeline Shop North Ipswich** (Riverlink Shopping Centre, North Ipswich). An
  in-mall Lifeline outlet. Best for combining a browse with a regular
  shopping-centre trip.
- **Salvos Store Riverlink** (Tenancy BG09, Riverlink Shopping Centre, cnr The
  Terrace & Downs St, North Ipswich). A second Salvos presence inside the same
  centre as the Lifeline shop above. Best for pairing the two in one stop without
  moving the car.

## Bundamba and Booval

- **Salvos Stores Bundamba** (12 Coal St, Bundamba). A standard charity op shop.
  Best for an everyday browse on the Bundamba side.
- **Wounded Heroes Op Shop** (41 Brisbane Rd, Bundamba). Run from the Wounded
  Heroes Australia Veterans Centre, with proceeds funding veteran crisis support.
  Best for a charity stop that directly backs veterans and their families.
- **One Man's Junk is Another One's Treasure** (18 Mining St, Bundamba). An
  independent secondhand shop rather than a national charity brand. Best for the
  unpredictable finds a chain store's sorting process tends to filter out.

## Redbank Plains and Springfield

- **Vinnies Redbank Plains** (Shop 42-43, Town Square Shopping Centre, 357
  Redbank Plains Rd, Redbank Plains). A standard charity op shop. Best for an
  everyday browse in the growth corridor.
- **Ipswich Community Care Fund Op Shop** (Shop 7B, Kruger Village, 183 Kruger
  Pde, Redbank Plains). An independent local charity running $2-shop pricing on
  most stock, including school uniforms, kids' clothes and books. Best for
  rock-bottom prices on everyday basics and school gear.
- **Vinnies Springfield** (10-22 Commercial Dr, Springfield). A standard charity
  op shop. Best for a charity browse without driving back toward the CBD.

## Goodna

- **Goodna Family Store** (27 Smiths Rd, Goodna). A Salvation Army family store
  run out of the Soundpoint Centre. Best for a full-category browse across
  clothing and household goods.

## Yamanto and Karalee

- **Salvos Stores Yamanto** (405 Warwick Rd, Yamanto). A standard charity op
  shop. Best for a southern-Ipswich browse on the Warwick Road run.
- **Vinnies Karalee** (39 Junction Rd, Karalee, in Karalee Shopping Village). A
  standard charity op shop in a small shopping village. Best for a quiet browse
  away from the CBD crowds.

## What to expect

The national charity brands dominate Ipswich by sheer volume — Vinnies, Salvos
and Lifeline account for most of the shops above, and the Brisbane Street core
alone puts four of them within walking distance. The independents and
cause-specific shops are the smaller but more interesting half of the mix, and
they cluster in the older suburbs closer to the CBD: the Wounded Heroes veterans'
shop and One Man's Junk at Bundamba, the $2-priced Ipswich Community Care Fund
shop at Redbank Plains. The growth-corridor centres are thinner — usually a
single Vinnies or Salvos anchoring a shopping centre, rather than a strip you can
walk.

## Tips for finding deals

- **Walk the Brisbane Street core.** Vinnies, Salvos and the AWL shop sit on the
  one street, with the Lifeline superstore at East Ipswich a short drive on. Four
  stops before you've really started.
- **Do Riverlink as one stop.** A Lifeline and a Salvos trade inside the same
  centre, so the second shop costs you nothing but the walk.
- **Go independent for the odd stuff.** Chain stores sort and price to a system.
  Bundamba's independents are where the mispriced and the unusual survive.

  [how to measure a garment guide](/guides/size-charts/#how-to-measure-a-garment)
  and [brand size charts](/guides/size-charts/) help when an item has no fitting
  room.
