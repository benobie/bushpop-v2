# Best Op Shops in Toowoomba

Updated 2026. Toowoomba's secondhand scene runs the length of Ruthven Street,
from the CBD down through South Toowoomba and Kearneys Spring, with a second
cluster of charity shops through Newtown and further outposts in Drayton, East
Toowoomba, Wilsonton and the southern suburbs. This is where to thrift across the
city, precinct by precinct, with addresses and a note on what each store does
best. Every shop below was checked for 2026.

## Toowoomba CBD

- **Orphans & Widows** (28 Russell St, Toowoomba City). A charity-run pre-loved
  shop known for brand-name clothing at affordable prices, a short walk from the
  bus terminal and train station. Best for brand-name finds without the trek out
  of the CBD.
- **ADRA Op Shop** (29 Bell St, Toowoomba City). An Adventist Development and
  Relief Agency op shop stocking secondhand clothing, toys, linen and — per one
  source — formal and bridal gowns. Best for a formalwear find alongside the
  everyday racks.
- **The Good Samaritan** (20 Bowen St, Toowoomba City). A volunteer-run Christian
  non-profit op shop, best for womenswear, cash only. Best for a womenswear-heavy
  browse away from the main strip.
- **Love Me Forever** (Shop 10, Hooper Centre Shopping Village, 187 Hume St,
  Toowoomba City). An independent high-end fashion consignment boutique — near-new
  designer clothing, jewellery, handbags and shoes, sizes 6-26, casual through to
  bridal. Best for designer consignment rather than a standard charity-shop
  browse.
- **Salvos Toowoomba Family Store** (10 Snell St, Toowoomba City). The Salvation
  Army's main Toowoomba retail store, selling fashion, furniture, homewares and
  books. Best for high-volume hunting across every category.

## Ruthven Street corridor

- **Red Cross Shop Toowoomba** (236 Ruthven St, Toowoomba). A more curated
  clothing selection than the average op shop, with new and preloved clothing at
  discount prices, notably open Sundays. Best for a weekend browse when most other
  shops are closed.
- **Lifeline Emporium** (302 Ruthven St, Toowoomba City). The single biggest
  vintage-clothing draw in the city — an entire floor dedicated to fashion, from
  $1 racks to vintage furs, plus an extensive book section (The Vintage Wardrobe &
  Bookshop). Best for the deepest vintage rummage in Toowoomba.
- **Save the Children Op Shop** (492 Ruthven St, Toowoomba City). A
  volunteer-staffed thrift shop selling donated clothing and bric-a-brac. Best for
  an everyday stop further down the Ruthven Street strip.

## South Toowoomba and Kearneys Spring

- **Vinnies Toowoomba (Madonna House)** (693-697 Ruthven St, South Toowoomba).
  St Vincent de Paul's flagship Toowoomba store across two levels — clothing,
  shoes and accessories upstairs, books, homewares and bric-a-brac below. Best for
  the biggest single-visit haul in the city.
- **Lifeline Perth Street Shop** (187 Perth St, South Toowoomba). Sells clothing,
  linen and bric-a-brac, with its own bookstore section. Best for pairing with the
  Madonna House Vinnies on the same South Toowoomba trip.
- **RSPCA Op Shop** (1/900 Ruthven St, Kearneys Spring). A combined op shop and
  cat-adoption centre, open all seven days. Best for a charity browse on a day
  every other shop is shut.

## Newtown

- **Lifeline Herries Street Superstore** (256 Herries St, Newtown). A large
  warehouse-format store with homewares, clothing, furniture and original art
  alongside the usual racks. Best for volume and variety in one visit.
- **Lifeline Wyalla Plaza Shop** (Wyalla Plaza, 238A Taylor St, Newtown). A
  suburban shopping-centre branch selling clothing, linen and bric-a-brac. Best
  for combining a browse with a Wyalla Plaza shopping trip.

## Drayton

- **Lifeline Anzac Avenue Shop** (Cnr Anzac Ave & Boothby St, Drayton). Known
  locally for its 50-cent bargain table, alongside clothing, linen and books. Best
  for rock-bottom prices on everyday basics.

## East Toowoomba

- **Lifeline The Range Boutique** (The Range Shopping Centre, 11 James St, East
  Toowoomba). A clothing-and-small-items specialty branch — furniture isn't
  accepted here. Best for pre-loved designer clothing and accessories specifically.

## Wilsonton

- **Lifeline Wilsonton Shop** (Shop T5, Wilsonton Shopping Centre, Cnr Bridge St &
  Richmond Dr, Wilsonton). A neighbourhood-centre branch selling clothing, linen
  and bric-a-brac. Best for an everyday browse in Wilsonton.

## Rangeville and Centenary Heights

- **Hope on a Hanger** (Shop 8, Cnr High St & Meibusch St, Rangeville). An op shop
  run for Hope Horizons, Toowoomba's cancer wellness centre — smaller and more
  curated, skewing toward mature-women's fashion. Best for a curated,
  higher-quality browse rather than a bulk rummage.
- **Drug ARM Quality Second Hand Shop** (Shop 16, Southtown Shopping Centre, 138
  South St, Centenary Heights). A volunteer-run op shop for the drug-and-alcohol
  harm-reduction charity, with broad stock across clothing, shoes, accessories and
  kids' items. Best for an everyday browse in the southern suburbs.

## What to expect

Ruthven Street is the spine of Toowoomba's op-shop scene — Red Cross, Lifeline
Emporium and Save the Children sit within a few blocks of each other in the CBD
stretch, with the Madonna House Vinnies and Lifeline Perth Street further south.
Lifeline runs the most branches across the city by far, so its stock varies most
by location — the Emporium leans vintage, The Range Boutique leans designer,
Herries Street leans volume.

## Tips for finding deals

- **Walk the Ruthven Street stretch.** Red Cross, Lifeline Emporium and Save the
  Children all sit on the one road through the CBD before it becomes South
  Toowoomba.
- **Check Lifeline Emporium for vintage first.** Its dedicated fashion floor,
  including vintage furs, is the deepest vintage stock in the city.
- **Know your measurements.** Sizing varies hugely across brands and eras. Our
  [how to measure a garment guide](/guides/size-charts/#how-to-measure-a-garment)
  and [brand size charts](/guides/size-charts/) help when an item has no fitting
  room.
