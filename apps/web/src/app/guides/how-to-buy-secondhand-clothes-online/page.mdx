# How to Buy Secondhand Clothes Online

Buying preloved fashion online is a different skill to buying new. There is no
fitting room, no staff member to ask, and every listing is written by a different
person with a different idea of what "good condition" means. None of that is a
reason to avoid it. Once you know what to look for, buying secondhand online is one
of the easiest ways to get better quality for less money, and to find things a
shop floor will never stock. This guide covers the basics: reading a listing
properly, judging whether a seller is trustworthy, protecting yourself on sizing,
and staying safe with payments.

## What to check in a listing

A good secondhand listing tells you almost everything you need to know before you
buy. A thin one should make you cautious.

- **Photos.** Look for multiple angles in natural light, including close-ups of
  any wear, marks, stitching or fabric texture. A seller who photographs flaws
  honestly, rather than hiding them in a dark corner shot, is a seller worth
  buying from.
- **Measurements, not just a size label.** The label says "size 12". The tape
  measure says what will actually fit you. Preloved and vintage sizing varies
  hugely between brands and decades, so treat the printed size as a rough guide
  and the garment's actual measurements as the real answer. See our guide on
  [getting the right fit without trying on](/guides/fit-without-trying-on/) for
  exactly how to compare your body to a garment's measurements.
- **Condition notes.** A specific description ("small mark on the left cuff, see
  photo four") is far more useful than a generic "good condition" with nothing to
  back it up. See the [condition-grading vocabulary](#condition-grading-vocabulary)
  below.
- **Fabric and material.** Knowing what something is made of tells you how it will
  fit, feel, drape and wash. A cotton tee behaves differently to a viscose blend;
  wool needs different care to polyester. If the listing does not state the
  fabric, it is a fair question to ask before you buy.

## Judging a seller and a listing for trustworthiness

Most secondhand sellers are genuine people clearing out wardrobes they no longer
wear, but it still pays to read a listing the way you would size up a market
stall.

- **Consistency.** Do the photos, description and measurements all describe the
  same item? Mismatches are a red flag.
- **Detail over hype.** A seller who writes "true to size, measured flat, chest
  52cm" is telling you something useful. A seller who only writes "gorgeous
  piece, will sell fast" is not.
- **Responsiveness.** A seller who answers a question about fit, fabric or postage
  clearly and promptly is generally a safer bet than one who does not reply at
  all.
- **Tracked postage.** Sellers who post with tracking give you visibility over
  where your order is, and give everyone a paper trail if something goes wrong in
  transit.

## Sizing risk, and how to reduce it

The single biggest source of disappointment in online secondhand shopping is
sizing, not condition. A labelled size from one brand, one country, or one decade
does not reliably match the same label from another. The fix is simple: compare
actual measurements, not size labels, every time. Our companion guide, [get the
right fit without trying on](/guides/fit-without-trying-on/), walks through how to
measure yourself and line that up against a garment's flat-lay measurements. Our
[size charts](/guides/size-charts/) guide also has a [how to measure a
garment](/guides/size-charts/#how-to-measure-a-garment) section with diagrams, plus
brand-by-brand charts for common Australian and international labels.

## Condition-grading vocabulary

Secondhand listings use a fairly consistent set of condition terms, but they are
not policed by anyone, so it helps to know roughly what each one should mean:

| Term | What it typically means |
|------|--------------------------|
| New with tags | Unworn, with the original retail tags still attached. |
| New without tags | Unworn and as-new, but the tags have been removed. |
| Excellent | Worn only a handful of times, with no visible flaws. |
| Very good | Light, even wear from occasional use, no notable faults. |
| Good | Visible wear consistent with regular use, with any flaws clearly disclosed. |
| Fair / worn | Well-loved, with noticeable wear or a specific flaw, usually priced to reflect it. |

Two items graded "good" by two different sellers can still look different in
person, because grading is a judgement call, not a standard. Treat the grade as a
starting point and the photos and written notes as the real evidence. Our [size
charts guide](/guides/size-charts/#condition-guide) covers this in more detail.

## Returns, and what if it doesn't fit

Every secondhand marketplace handles returns and disputes a little differently,
so it is worth checking a platform's policy before you buy rather than assuming it
matches a retail return. As a general rule across secondhand platforms:

- **Message the seller first.** Most sizing and condition questions are sorted
  quickly through a direct conversation.
- **Check what is covered.** Genuine problems (an item that never arrives, or is
  significantly different to how it was described) are usually covered by a
  platform's buyer protection. A simple change of mind, or a fit issue where the
  seller's measurements were accurate, usually is not.
- **Know the timeframe.** Most platforms expect you to report a problem within a
  set number of days of delivery, so check an item as soon as it arrives.

## Payment and platform safety

The same commonsense rules that apply to any online marketplace apply to
secondhand fashion:

- **Never pay outside the platform.** If a seller asks you to pay by bank
  transfer, gift card or a different app "to save on fees", that is the clearest
  warning sign there is. Paying outside a platform usually means you lose any
  buyer protection that platform offers.
- **Keep the conversation on-platform.** It creates a record of what was agreed,
  which matters if there is ever a dispute.
- **Be wary of urgency.** "Pay right now or I'll sell it to someone else" is a
  pressure tactic, not a reason to skip your usual checks.
- **Don't share more than you need to.** A seller needs your shipping address to
  post an item. They do not need your bank login, card PIN or one-time codes.
