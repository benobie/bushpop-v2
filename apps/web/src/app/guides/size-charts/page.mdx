## How to measure a garment

When you are buying secondhand, the size on the label matters less than the actual
measurements. Sizing drifts between brands, between eras, and after years of
washing. The most reliable way to know if something will fit is to measure a piece
you already own and love, then compare it to the listing. Sellers should measure
every item flat and include the numbers in the description.

Whatever you're measuring — a top, a pair of bottoms, a dress or a pair of
shoes — the same three steps apply:

1. Lay the item flat on a hard surface and smooth out any wrinkles.
2. Measure straight across (never around a curve), in centimetres.
3. For circumference measurements — bust, waist, hip — double the flat
   measurement to get the full circumference.

| Measurement | How to take it |
|-------------|----------------|
| Bust / chest | Measure straight across from one underarm seam to the other, then double it for the full circumference. |
| Waist | Measure straight across the narrowest point, or along the waistband, then double it. |
| Hips | Measure straight across the widest point of the lower half, then double it. |
| Length (tops and dresses) | Measure from the highest point of the shoulder straight down to the hem. |
| Length (bottoms) | Measure from the top of the waistband down the outside leg to the hem. |
| Rise | Measure from the top of the waistband straight down to the crotch seam. |
| Inseam | Measure from the crotch seam straight down to the bottom of the leg. |
| Sleeve | Measure from the shoulder seam to the end of the cuff. |
| Shoulder | Measure from one shoulder seam straight across the back to the other. |
| Foot length (insole) | Place the shoe on paper, trace around the sole, then measure heel to toe in a straight line. |

A quick tip: keep a tape measure where you store your clothes. Measuring the item
you reach for most often gives you a personal benchmark to match against any
listing, which is far more accurate than guessing from a size label.

## Condition guide

Secondhand condition is described on a simple scale. Knowing what each grade means
helps buyers set expectations and helps sellers price honestly. On Bushpop, an
accurate condition note and clear photos of any flaws are the best protection for
both sides of a sale.

| Condition | What it means |
|-----------|---------------|
| New with tags | Unworn, with the original retail tags still attached. |
| New without tags | Unworn and as-new, but the tags have been removed. |
| Excellent | Worn only a handful of times, with no visible flaws. |
| Very good | Light, even wear from occasional use, no notable faults. |
| Good | Visible wear consistent with regular use; any flaws are clearly disclosed. |
| Fair | Well-loved, with significant wear or a disclosed flaw, priced to reflect it. |

Whatever the grade, the golden rule is the same: describe condition honestly and
photograph any flaws up close. For a full walk-through of listing, pricing and
photographing your items, see the
[complete guide to selling on Bushpop](/selling/how-to-sell-on-bushpop-the-complete-guide/).
